## Implementation notes

### Chunk 1 — powered parts (CP-1 … CP-5)

**How the drive train is wired.** Engines, motors, clutches, gearboxes, differentials, brakes and drive shafts never refer to each other by id. Each names *shafts* ("crank", "driveshaft", "left" …) and, every fixed step, registers what it does to them through `ctx.drive` (`src/physics/components/drive/network.ts`): `inertia(shaft, I)` for rotating mass, `torque(shaft, T0, b)` for a source `T = T0 − b·ω'` (implicit in the damping, so back-EMF droop and drag are stable at any step), and rows — `gear`, `couple`, `drive`, `limit` — that relate shaft speeds, optionally bounded by a torque (a gear ratio, a locked differential, a slipping clutch, a brake, a travel stop). After every component has registered, `solve()` finds all new shaft speeds at once with the direct active-set solver in `drive/solver.ts` (dense solve over the bounded rows, projected Gauss–Seidel as a fallback) and hands the impulse back to the physical world as ±J on the two bodies of each hinge or slider. A shaft is *virtual* (named flywheel with its own saved state: crank, gearbox shafts) or *physical* (a hinge / slider joint: speed measured from the bodies, inertia measured with the parallel-axis theorem — `drive/bindings.ts`). Shaft names are scoped to a jointed assembly (`@name` is scene-wide). A fresh shaft starts in a consistent state (`initialise()` solves the rigid rows with the bodies frozen) so a wheel that already spins does not exchange momentum with its flywheel on the first step; virtual shaft speeds are saved and restored with a snapshot (`'@drive'`). Registration order is the deterministic component order, so a restore replays bit-identically (`tests/powered.car.test.ts`).

**Which parts use the network and which use the engine's joint motor.** Engine, motor, brake, clutch, gearbox, differential and drive shaft use the network (a drive train through a gearbox and a differential into two wheels is one solve). Servo, stepper, linear actuator and hydraulic cylinder configure the joint's own implicit motor instead (`JointBinding.motorVelocity`, gain 1e5 rot / 1e8 lin, force limit = the part's torque / force / pressure × area): the engine's solver sees the whole mechanism behind the joint (an arm with a payload, a crane boom with a cylinder), so they hold loads and respect limits at any gain, which an impulse sized from two bodies' own inertia cannot. Their torque / force gauges are estimates because the motor force cannot be read back.

**Variable mass.** `ctx.body.setMass(kg)` (`src/physics/world/massTrim.ts`) sets the total mass with the centre of mass kept and the inertia scaled; the thruster and jet use it with a `Fuel` helper (`powered/fuel.ts`) that burns half a step's fuel on each side of the force, which makes the rocket equation exact to ~1e-4 (the engine keeps the new mass through snapshots). Thrust geometry is `powered/nozzle.ts`; `powered/rotary.ts` has the shared "spin from a shaft, a hinge or by itself" logic of propellers and rotors; `powered/position.ts` the shared target-following of servo and actuators.

**Controls and effects.** `src/model/controls.ts` finds every key (`key` fields, joint-motor keys) and analog axis (`text` fields with `role: 'axis'`) of the enabled components, so a new component appears in the **Controls** tab of the bottom dock (`src/ui/controlsPanel.ts`) without any UI code: a slider per axis (spring-back option, double-click zeroes it) and a key cap per key that holds the key for the simulation like the keyboard does. A text field with `role: 'axis'` / `'shaft'` suggests the names already used in the scene. Flames, blade blur and gauges are decided by `src/render/componentFxPlan.ts` (pure, tested in Node: a teardrop flame per thruster / jet that follows the thrust actually produced, blade discs that fade into a blur with rpm and lean with the rotor's cyclic, dials for servo / stepper angle against target and travel, stroke markers for actuators and cylinders, a brake ring that glows with the pedal; faint previews for the selected owner while editing) and drawn by `src/render/componentFx.ts` (pooled by component id). Thrusters raise `ignite` / `burnout` events: the hooks for sound and particles.

**Example scenes and prefabs (one per family).** Powertrain car (engine → clutch → 3-speed auto gearbox → open / lockable differential → brakes, parking brake, reverse), engine test stand, electric rover (differential drive), servo arm, stepper turntable, lift table (linear actuator on an axis), hydraulic crane, rocket launch, jet car, propeller cart (engine → shaft → propeller), helicopter (collective, cyclic, tail rotor). **Verified (evidence per item).**

* CP-1 — `tests/powered.engine.test.ts` (6): a free engine on a flat 100 N·m curve with a 0.25 kg·m² flywheel revs at exactly α = T/I = 400 rad/s²; two throttle levels diverge where the *curve* says (the speed is set by the data, not the code); the rev limiter caps at 4000 rpm with a fuel-cut ramp and engine braking at closed throttle; the idle governor holds ≈ 900 rpm; on a hinge the wheel accelerates at T/(I_wheel + I_flywheel) and the reaction goes to the other body; a 3:1 reduction multiplies torque and divides speed. The torque curve is editable in the inspector (curve editor, `e2e/tests/powered.mjs` screenshot `powered-06-inspector.png`). `tests/driveSolver.test.ts` (6) checks the solver on its own (gear pairs, slipping clutch, brake stopping exactly, a 0.001 : 50 kg·m² stiff chain). The powertrain car drives through the whole chain (`tests/powered.car.test.ts`: accelerates and auto-shifts 1→2→3, brakes to a stop, parking brake holds, reverses, snapshot replay identical).
* CP-2 — `tests/powered.actuators.test.ts`: motor ω(t) = ω₀(1 − e^(−t/τ)) with τ = I·ω₀/T_stall (37.9 of 60 rad/s at t = τ), supply-limit plateau at T_max/I (19.6 rad/s after 1 s), half drive settles at half speed from a key / axis; servo reaches its target and stops, never exceeds the speed cap (time = distance ÷ cap), holds against gravity inside its torque limit and sags beyond it, jog keys and an axis map onto the travel range; stepper moves one step per press, a pulse train turns at pulses × step angle, deflection under load e = step·load/T_hold, loses steps when the load exceeds the holding torque and stalls past the pull-in rate.
* CP-3 — same file: linear actuator reaches the target within mm at no more than its speed limit, 98 N lifts a 10 kg load and 50 N does not, stops at its travel limits and an axis maps onto the range; hydraulic cylinder lifts at flow ÷ area with force = load weight, a load beyond pressure × area stalls it at exactly that force, the retract stroke is weaker by the rod area, a centred valve holds the load up to the relief pressure and floats when open, stops at the stroke ends.
* CP-4 — `tests/powered.drivetrain.test.ts` (17): brake decelerates at T/I and stops exactly (no creep), holds below its torque and slips above it, an analog pedal scales the torque, the parking brake latches; clutch slips at its capacity then locks where angular momentum says (momentum-conserving speed), the pedal disengages it; gearbox α_wheel = r·T/(I_wheel + I_shaft + r²·I_input), shift keys pass through neutral and reverse, auto mode shifts on rpm thresholds, a shift interrupts the drive for the shift time; differential open (equal torque, ω_in = (ω_L + ω_R)/2, a speed difference persists in a corner), locked (one speed, torque splits by inertia), lock key, limited slip closes at T/I_reduced; shaft names are private per assembly, `@name` is global, and the full engine → clutch → gearbox → differential → two wheels chain pulls a car-like load and stays finite.
* CP-5 — `tests/powered.thrust.test.ts` (25): rocket equation Δv = v_e·ln(m₀/m₁) with v_e = thrust·burn time ÷ fuel, mass m(t) = m₀ − ṁt, with gravity v = v_e ln(m₀/m) − g t; half throttle gives half thrust and half fuel flow at the same v_e; liquid motors are held with a key, solid ones burn to the end; an offset nozzle turns the body (τ = r × F); `ignite` / `burnout` events; snapshot replay identical (mass included); jet spools as 1 − e^(−t/τ), afterburner only near full speed, thrust × (1 − (v/v_max)²), fuel is real mass until flame-out; propeller thrust ∝ rpm² (a quarter at half speed), thrust falls with the advance ratio, an engine through a shaft settles where engine torque = C_Q ρ n² D⁵, torque reaction turns a self-driven body; rotor lift C_T·collective·ρπR²(ΩR)², hover at the weight-sized collective, collective by keys / axis, cyclic leans the thrust (L sin θ forward, L cos θ up), hub lever and rotor head moment, drag torque. The launch scene (`tests/powered.content.test.ts`) follows the rocket equation to within 0.3 m/s over a 12 s burn from a pad. Flames, blade blur and gauges: `tests/powered.fx.test.ts` (7, over the real world), e2e screenshots `powered-02-rocket-firing.png`, `powered-05-helicopter.png`, `powered-07-jet-exhaust.png`, `powered-08-jet-afterburner.png`, `powered-09-propeller-blur.png`, `powered-04-servo-arm.png`.
* Across the chunk — `tests/powered.robust.test.ts` (7): motor against brake on one shaft (the stronger wins, live edit flips it), removing wheels, hinges and the engine mid-drive, misplaced components (a servo on a ball joint, a gearbox with no shafts) do nothing wrong, every powered def has a runtime and defaults that survive their own sanitising, a body carrying all of them simulates without a component error; `tests/powered.controls.test.ts` (6) for the Controls scan; `e2e/tests/powered.mjs` (7) drives the real UI: slider sets the throttle (450 N at +0.50), holding the F key cap fires the rocket and a flame is drawn, the car drives with the real keyboard and its engine card shows live rpm, the servo arm jogs, the helicopter lifts off, the Add menu offers the powered parts and a shaft field suggests names, the jet and propeller carts draw their exhaust and blur.
