# 01 · Shapes, models, colliders, terrain (package SH)

Depends on: foundation (shape registry, compound bodies, asset store). Every shape: inspector parameter editing, correct Rapier collider (convex/compound/trimesh as appropriate), correct volume → mass, bounding radius, three.js geometry, palette tile with a generated icon/thumbnail, serialisation round-trip, physics test (mass = density × analytic volume within tolerance; rests stably on ground; rolls/tumbles plausibly) and a render check.

## Implementation notes

### Chunk 1 — primitive shapes I (SH-1 .. SH-6)

* **One file per shape** under `src/model/shapes/`, registered after the original eight in `shapes/index.ts` (palette order; the 1–9 keys are unchanged). Shared code: `meshKit.ts` (vertex / triangle collector with **crease-angle normals**, `lathe`, `extrudeOutline` with an ear-clipping triangulator, uvs on everything), `parts.ts` (`boxPart`, `hullPart`, `prismPoints`), `revolve.ts` (convex solids of revolution), `annulus.ts` (ring + tube), `beamSection.ts` (I / L / T), `shapeIcons.ts` (inline SVG icons, so `ui/icons.ts` is untouched). The geometry factories use only the existing `GeometryKit` (`BufferGeometry` + `Float32BufferAttribute`), so no shared type changed.
* **Compound colliders tile the solid, they never overlap**, so the engine's summed mass equals `density × volume()` and `volume()` is an exact closed form of the *collider polyhedron*, not of the smooth ideal. The trick that makes the closed forms exact: consecutive slices of a ring (torus, washer, pipe) have end faces that are mirror images in the slice's bisecting plane, so every side face of the slice's hull is a planar trapezoid and the slice volume is `sin(2π/N) · A · R`; rings of aligned regular N-gons (hemisphere, dome, frustum) are stacks of pyramid frusta, `h/3 (A₁ + A₂ + √(A₁A₂))`. The mesh is built from the *same* polygons, and a unit test integrates each mesh's signed volume and compares it with `volume()` (outward winding and the formula in one check). Consequence: at the defaults a torus (24 × 16) is 3.7 % lighter than the smooth torus `2π²Rr²`; at 64 × 32 it is 0.8 % lighter.
* **Size = bounding box, parameters = ratios.** `halfHeight` is always `size.y / 2`, hole / wall / thickness / flange / web are fractions of a size so the scale gizmo resizes a shape as a whole; `normalizeSize` keeps shapes valid (torus tube ≤ 40 % of the outer diameter so a hole stays open, hemisphere height = radius, dome 5–50 %, arch tall enough for its opening). Meshes are built in a unit frame and shared by `geometryKey` (equal ratio and counts share one geometry whatever the size).
* **The arch's top is one cuboid.** A first version with the deck split into slabs kicked a rolling 0.1 m ball by up to 1.4 cm at the seams (ghost collisions between coplanar convex parts); one deck box rolls like a plain box (`tests/primitiveShapes.test.ts › "a ball rolls the length of the deck …"`).
* **Spiral stairs:** a tread's inner edge is the chord of the column circle pushed out until it is tangent to it, so a tread never overlaps the (exact cylinder) column and nothing is counted twice; its outer edge is a polyline with ≤ 15° per piece. `stepsPerTurn ≥ 6` keeps every tread convex.
* Honest approximations are listed in `docs/LIMITATIONS.md` (“Shapes”).
* e2e: `e2e/tests/shapes1.mjs` adds every shape from the Add tab, checks the inspector rows, edits parameters (undoable), renders a gallery and parameter variants (screenshots `sh1-*.png` looked at), and simulates the torus / arch / stairs / spiral / dynamic bodies in the real worker.

### Chunk 2 — primitive shapes II and the palette (SH-7 .. SH-13)

* **New shapes, one file each** under `src/model/shapes/`: `gear`, `wheel`, `ellipsoid`, `pyramid` (+ `tetrahedron`), `platonicShapes` (octahedron, dodecahedron), `roundedBox`, `roundedCylinder`, `curvedRamp` (+ `quarter-pipe`), `halfPipe`, `dice` (d4 d6 d8 d10 d12 d20); registered after chunk 1 in `shapes/index.ts` (the first nine, and so the 1–9 keys, are unchanged). Shared code: `hull3.ts` (quickhull, hull volume / faces / safety rule), `convexSolid.ts` (a `ShapeDef` from unit points, affine or absolute sizes), `polyMesh.ts` (analytic normals and uvs), `lattice.ts`, `bevel.ts`, `latheMesh.ts`, `profileSolid.ts` (extruded profiles for the ramps and pipes), `platonic.ts`, `diceLayout.ts` / `diceMarks.ts` / `roundedDie.ts`, `keywords.ts`, `memo.ts`. Every shape is still checked by the generic registry test, and each has its own mass / mesh / physics tests.
* **The engine's convex-hull builder is the hard constraint.** Rapier's builder (parry) merges faces whose normals differ by less than about 1.35° and mis-handles vertices closer than about 0.1–0.25 % of the size to the plane of a polygonal face; the integrated mass is then wrong by up to tens of percent (found while building the rounded cylinder: 30 %). `hull3.ts` encodes what was measured over a corpus of thousands of hulls — `ENGINE_MIN_DIHEDRAL = 2.5°` between a polygon face (4 or more corners) and its neighbours, `ENGINE_CLEARANCE = 0.0015` of the bounding-box diagonal — and `firstSafeHull` takes candidate point sets from the most detailed to a plain one and returns the first the engine reproduces. `tests/engineHull.test.ts` proves the rule against the real engine (no accepted hull is mis-integrated; the rejected ones are where the engine goes wrong) and then that every shape built on it has engine mass = `density × volume()` over parameter grids. Triangle-only hulls (the ellipsoid lattice) are not affected.
* **Mass is exact for the polyhedron**, as in chunk 1: `volume()` is the volume of the hull the collider and the mesh both use, and the mesh volume is integrated in the tests. Engine rounding shapes (`RoundCuboid`) would be a lighter, rounder, wrong-mass alternative: parry ignores the border when it computes mass properties (a rounded box would weigh 1.9× too little), so the rounded shapes are hulls.
* **Affine and absolute shapes.** Gear, wheel, pyramid, the regular solids, dice and ramps build one mesh per parameter set and scale it by `visualScale`. The rounded box and the ellipsoid have absolute parameters (a corner radius is metres) and build the mesh at its real size with `visualScale [1,1,1]`; their `geometryKey` includes the size.
* **Shared edits kept small**, each marked `// -- SH`: `ShapeDef.keywords / quantities / surface` and the `completeSpec` / `applyQuantity` helpers in `shapeRegistry.ts`; the world's contact prediction distance (below); two lines in `bodyViews.ts` (vertex colours and painted surfaces through `render/shapeSurface.ts`); one call in `libraryPanels.ts` (the new palette) and one in `inspector/body.ts` (quantity rows); the palette assertion in `e2e/tests/foundation.mjs`.
* **Contact prediction distance 2 mm (was 2 cm).** The engine's default makes a ball sliding or rolling over a surface made of several colliders touch the *edge of the next piece* before reaching it, and those speculative contacts have normals that steal speed (a frictionless ball on a 30° slope of 16 coplanar slabs kept 70 % of its speed). `src/physics/world/settings.ts` now sets `normalizedPredictionDistance = 0.002`: the same ball keeps 97 % over 1, 8, 16 and 32 slabs at 60, 120 and 240 Hz (`tests/seams.test.ts`). 1 mm broke the "Sandbox box" map test, so 2 mm it is. This improves the seams of chunk 1's shapes too.
* **Dice.** The d4–d20 are regular solids resting on a face (the d10 the pentagonal trapezohedron), so every face is equivalent by symmetry; fairness is measured, not assumed (3 168 rolls in the engine, χ² against the p = 0.001 critical value for every die). Their numbers come from a **face atlas**: `diceLayout.ts` gives every face a cell, an upright frame and the uvs; `diceMarks.ts` computes pips / numbers / d4 corner labels (largest single font size that fits the tightest face, each label at the roomiest spot, 6 and 9 underlined); `render/dicePainter.ts` only draws that. The atlas is an *ink mask* (black = body, white = ink); `render/shapeSurface.ts` patches the standard material to mix dark or light ink (by the body colour's luminance) into the colour, so any die colour and the colour modes keep working. The d6 has its own hull (`roundedDie.ts`): a faceted rounding can leave an edge or a corner facet under the centre of mass — a die that rests on an edge — so its edges have an even number of facets and its corners a vertex on the diagonal, its radius is limited to a quarter of the edge, and the test `restingFaces` finds exactly six faces at 51 roundness values (a first version with the general rounded-box lattice came to rest on an edge in 7 of 360 rolls).
* **Palette.** `ui/shapePalette.ts` (+ `ui/shapeSearch.ts`, pure and unit-tested, `styles/shapePalette.css`). Categories come from `ShapeDef.category` (`Solids` is the "regular solids" group of the checklist); searching shows a flat, ranked result list; collapse state is kept in localStorage. **Thumbnails** (`render/thumbnails.ts`, `thumbCache.ts`): drawn offscreen in batches of 12 into one canvas and cut apart on the CPU (reading a WebGL canvas back waits for the graphics process, so one read per batch rather than per shape — the difference between 40 s and 2 s under software rendering), with one long-lived material per surface kind (the engine deletes a shader program when its last material is disposed, and recompiling it per thumbnail was the other big cost), cached in memory and in localStorage under `physicslab.shapeThumb.<id>` with a fingerprint (thumbnail style version, geometry key, default size, volume) so an edited shape or style is redrawn, the context released 1.5 s after the last one; without WebGL the icons stay.
* **Inspector.** Size fields, parameters and the new *quantities* (`ShapeDef.quantities`: a gear's *Module* and *Pitch diameter*, the rounded shapes' *Corner radius* / *Rim radius*) are generic rows; editing a running body's shape goes through `updateBody` (the engine body is rebuilt in place at its live pose and velocity). `tests/shapeLiveEdit.test.ts` drives every field of every shape through that path.
* **Tests and e2e.** Unit: `hull3`, `engineHull`, `solids2`, `rounded`, `ramps`, `seams`, `gear`, `wheel`, `dice`, `diceMarks`, `shapeLiveEdit`, `shapeSearch` (and the thumbnail cache). e2e: `e2e/tests/shapes2.mjs` — palette (categories, thumbnails cached across a reload, collapse memory, number keys), search and keyboard navigation, the gallery of all 18 new shapes (textured dice, dark tyre), inspector rows and quantity edits, live resizing in a running scene, the scale gizmo path, and six dice rolled in the browser worker; screenshots `sh2-*.png` (E2E_SHOTS=1) were looked at.
